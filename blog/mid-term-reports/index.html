When to Book a Mid-Term Report for Rental Properties

While Check-In and Check-Out reports act as the bookends of a tenancy, a Mid-Term Inspection (or Interim Report) serves as the vital check-up in between. It is a proactive measure that helps landlords and letting agents ensure their investment is secure and allows tenants to officially raise any maintenance concerns. But when is the right time to book one?

The Ideal Frequency

Industry best practice generally dictates that a mid-term inspection should occur:

  • Every 6 months for long-term tenancies.
  • Around the 3rd month for shorter (e.g., 6-month) tenancies, giving you a chance to assess the situation before deciding whether to offer a renewal.
  • Sooner if there have been past issues with the tenant, or if the property has experienced significant recent repairs (such as a new boiler installation or major plumbing work) that need monitoring.

Why Are Mid-Term Inspections Essential?

1. Spotting Maintenance Issues Early

A small leak under the kitchen sink might seem insignificant to a tenant, so they might not report it. However, over six months, that slow drip can cause severe rot, mould, and structural damage to the cabinetry and flooring. A mid-term inspection catches these unreported maintenance issues early, turning what could be a massive repair bill into a quick, inexpensive fix.

2. Identifying Lease Breaches

Unfortunately, some tenancies do not go as planned. A mid-term report is the best way to verify that the tenant is adhering to the tenancy agreement. Common breaches uncovered during these inspections include:

  • Unauthorised pets: Spotting signs like lingering odours, scratched doors, or pet hair.
  • Subletting: Identifying extra beds or locks on interior doors that suggest more people are living there than permitted.
  • Smoking indoors: Checking for nicotine stains or smells in properties where smoking is prohibited.

3. Checking Health and Safety Compliance

Landlords have strict legal obligations regarding the safety of their properties. During an interim visit, a clerk will typically check that smoke alarms and carbon monoxide detectors are still present, functioning, and haven't been tampered with or had their batteries removed.
