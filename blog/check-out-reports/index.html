What to Expect from a Check Out Report at the End of a Tenancy

The end of a tenancy can be a busy and stressful time. For landlords, the priority is getting the property ready for the next let. For tenants, it's about moving to a new home and getting their deposit back. The Check-Out report is the crucial final step that determines how smoothly this transition happens. Here is what you can expect from the process.

The Role of the Original Inventory

A Check-Out report does not exist in isolation; it is a direct comparison against the original Check-In Inventory. An independent clerk will visit the property on the last day of the tenancy (after all personal belongings have been removed and final cleaning is complete) with the original report in hand. They will systematically go through the property, noting any changes, damages, or missing items.

Understanding "Fair Wear and Tear"

The most debated aspect of any Check-Out is the concept of "fair wear and tear." Tenants cannot be held responsible for the natural deterioration of a property over time. Adjudicators take several factors into account when determining what constitutes fair wear and tear:

  • Length of tenancy: A carpet will naturally show more wear after three years than after six months.
  • Number and age of occupants: A family of five will cause more natural wear than a single professional.
  • Quality of the items: Cheap, budget furnishings are expected to wear out much faster than premium, durable goods.

A professional clerk is trained to differentiate between natural wear (e.g., slight fading of curtains, minor scuffs on hallway walls) and actual damage or negligence (e.g., a red wine stain on the carpet, a cracked window, or a broken cupboard hinge).

Cleaning Standards

Cleaning is consistently the number one cause of deposit disputes in the UK. The general rule is that the property must be returned in the same standard of cleanliness as it was provided, as documented in the Check-In report. If the oven was professionally cleaned at the start, it must be returned professionally cleaned. The Check-Out report will meticulously detail the cleanliness of every area, including commonly missed spots like the inside of the washing machine seal, the extractor fan filters, and the tops of cupboards.

Final Meter Readings and Key Handover

As part of the Check-Out process, the clerk will take final meter readings (gas, electric, and water) and photograph them. This is essential for closing utility accounts accurately and preventing the incoming tenant or the landlord from being charged for the outgoing tenant's usage. The clerk will also collect and document all sets of keys returned by the tenant.

The Final Document

Once completed, the Check-Out report is typically sent to both the landlord (or letting agent) and the tenant within 48 hours. It provides a clear, photographic, and entirely objective summary of the property's final condition, making any necessary deposit negotiations transparent and fact-based.
